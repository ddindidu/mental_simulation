Ground truth: Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder (Combined Presentation)
The patient meets the diagnostic criteria for Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity Disorder, Combined Presentation (F90.2). He presents with a clear, chronic history of both inattentive symptoms (distractibility, difficulty sustaining focus during tasks, and organization issues) and hyperactive-impulsive symptoms (internal restlessness, difficulty remaining seated, interrupting others, and impatience). These symptoms have been present since childhood (elementary school, well before age 12) and currently cause significant functional impairment in both his occupational and home life. Generalized Anxiety Disorder (F41.1) was considered due to his reported worry, but this was ruled out as a primary diagnosis because his anxiety is secondary to his ADHD-related functional difficulties.

Doctor’s diagnostic checklist

inattention (4)
  • Easily distracted by external stimuli (mind jumps to every little thing around him)
  • Difficulty sustaining attention in tasks (losing track of what he is doing during customer calls)
  • Making careless mistakes and rushing through tasks (noted since elementary school)
  • Difficulty organizing tasks and activities at work and home
hyperactivity_impulsivity (6)
  • Fidgeting with or tapping hands or feet (pencil tapping since childhood)
  • Difficulty remaining seated when required (struggles to sit through long meetings)
  • Subjective feelings of restlessness (internal buzzing feeling, strong urge to pace)
  • Talking excessively (constantly talking in class as a child)
  • Blurting out answers or finishing other people's sentences
  • Difficulty waiting his turn (impatience during team calls)
